Accuracy predictions were within ~1%: the substitution logic (replacing
a dominated 1T model preserves/improves accuracy) holds reliably.

Latency predictions were 1.5–4x too optimistic. They assumed the mean
would stay near the T1 latency since T1 handles ~78% of tasks. The error
grows with T3 model size because the prediction ignored how expensive
the remaining ~10% of tasks are when they hit a thinking model.

--- Why latency predictions failed ---

The prediction treated the cascade as a black-box replacement for a
1T model: "if cascade X has mean latency L and dominates 1T model Y,
then swapping Y for X in a 2T config should give latency ≈ L."

This ignores that mean latency is dominated by tail costs:

  Config           T1 pass  T3 pass  T3 LLM lat   T3 E2E      Fail E2E
  ···············  ·······  ·······  ··········   ··········  ··········
  4nt→8nt→14nt     78.9%     6.9%      9.8s       35.2s       20.0s
  4nt→8nt→1.7t     78.9%     4.7%     33.7s       37.8s       48.6s
  4nt→8nt→4t       78.2%     6.9%     70.3s       63.3s      181.8s
  4nt→8nt→8t       77.6%     6.5%     93.1s       84.7s      196.6s
  4nt→8nt→14t      77.0%     9.6%    163.9s      141.0s      364.9s

Thinking models produce long reasoning chains on hard tasks (the exact
tasks that stumped T1 and T2). Even though T3 only fires on ~10-20% of
tasks, its per-invocation cost (70–164s LLM time) pulls the mean up
dramatically. Nothink 14nt as T3 costs only 9.8s LLM time — 7-17x
cheaper per invocation than thinking models.

--- Key insight: think model T3 latency scales superlinearly ---

  T3 model    T3 LLM lat    Acc gain over 4nt→8nt (90.8%)    Lat/acc ratio
  ·········   ··········    ····························      ·············
  14nt           9.8s       +3.9% (→94.7%)                    1.7s per 1%
  1.7t          33.7s       +2.4% (→93.3%)                    3.5s per 1%
  4t            70.3s       +5.9% (→96.8%)                    5.5s per 1%
  8t            93.1s       +5.3% (→96.1%)                    6.8s per 1%
  14t          163.9s       +6.9% (→97.8%)                   10.5s per 1%

14nt gives the best accuracy per latency-second. Thinking models pay
exponentially more latency for diminishing accuracy gains. 8t is
strictly worse than 4t (less accurate AND slower) — suggesting that
8B-think's longer chains don't help on tasks that stumped 4B-nothink
and 8B-nothink.

--- Nothink T2 choice matters: 8nt vs 14nt ---

  4nt→8nt →4t    T2_pass=11.6%  T3_pass=6.9%  lat=13.7s
  4nt→14nt→4t    T2_pass=15.8%  T3_pass=2.9%  lat=11.8s

14nt as T2 solves 4.2% more tasks than 8nt, leaving fewer for the
expensive 4t T3. Result: same accuracy, 14% lower latency. The
latency cost of running 14nt (10.7s) vs 8nt (4.4s) per T2 invocation
is more than offset by avoiding expensive T3 invocations.

--- Lessons for 4T extension ---

1. ACCURACY PREDICTIONS ARE RELIABLE. The compositional substitution
   logic (replace dominated model → preserve accuracy) works. Trust
   accuracy estimates within ~1%.

2. LATENCY PREDICTIONS ARE NOT RELIABLE for thinking models. Do not
   estimate 4T latency by summing cascade latency + rescue latency.
   Instead, expect thinking T3/T4 costs to dominate the mean whenever
   they fire on >5% of tasks.

3. ALL-NOTHINK CASCADES WIN ON EFFICIENCY. 4nt→8nt→14nt achieves
   94.7% at 6.7s — better accuracy/latency ratio than any cascade
   using thinking models. Use nothink for the cheap early turns.

4. THINKING MODELS BELONG IN THE FINAL TURN ONLY. Their value is
   solving the hardest remaining tasks, but their per-task cost is
   extreme (70-164s). Placing them earlier wastes their expensive
   reasoning on tasks that cheaper models could handle.

5. LARGER NOTHINK T2 > SMALLER NOTHINK T2. Spending more on T2 (14nt
   vs 8nt) pays back by reducing expensive T3 invocations.

6. 8B-THINK IS DOMINATED. 4nt→8nt→8t (96.1%, 16.7s) is strictly
   worse than 4nt→8nt→4t (96.8%, 13.7s). 8B-think's longer chains
   don't help on hard tasks and cost more. Avoid 8t in cascades.

7. EXPECT DIMINISHING RETURNS FROM 4T. If 3T only nudges the 2T
   frontier by thin margins, 4T will likely do the same relative to
   3T. The biggest 4T wins will come from all-nothink early turns
   (Groups A, C) rather than adding more thinking models (Group B).

--- Assumption Check ---

1. "4T WILL ONLY NUDGE 3T BY THIN MARGINS" is only partly true.
   - True for P50: only 1 of 13 4T configs reaches the combined P50 frontier.
   - Mostly true for P90: only 2 of 13 4T configs survive there.
   - False for mean latency: 4T adds 5 frontier points and moves the
     frontier sharply from 94.7% at 6.7s (3T) to 98.2% at 10.5s (4T).

2. "ALL-NOTHINK EARLY TURNS ARE THE BACKBONE" holds.
   The strongest 4T mean/P90 points all keep cheap nothink turns up
   front. The best examples are:
     - 4nt→4nt→8nt→14nt      : 95.9%, 10.2s mean
     - 4nt→8nt→14nt→4t       : 98.2%, 10.5s mean
     - 4nt→8nt→14nt→14t      : 98.8%, 13.1s p90 / 17.1s mean
     - 1.7nt→4nt→14nt→14t    : 98.6%, 17.0s mean

3. "THINKING MODELS BELONG IN THE FINAL TURN ONLY" mostly holds for
   mean and P90, but not perfectly for P50.
   - Mean/P90: middle-think configs are usually dominated.
       4nt→8nt→1.7t→4t is dominated on mean by 1.7nt→4nt→14nt→14t.
       1.7nt→4nt→4t→14t is strictly worse than 1.7nt→4nt→14nt→14t
       at the same 98.6% accuracy.
   - P50: 4nt→8nt→1.7t→14t is actually the only 4T P50 frontier point.
     This does not contradict the mean result. P50 only sees the
     typical request, and the median task exits before T3/T4.

4. "ACCURACY PREDICTIONS ARE RELIABLE" only holds for the successful
   substitution families.
   - Group C / D behaved as expected: stronger nothink prefixes plus a
     sparse rescue turn raised accuracy substantially.
   - Group B did not reliably preserve parent accuracy:
       14nt→4t               = 97.0%, 17.4s mean
       4nt→8nt→1.7t→4t       = 96.3%, 19.0s mean
     Replacing a strong nothink parent with a weaker think middle turn
     can lose accuracy as well as speed.

5. "GROUP A PREFIX SUBSTITUTION WILL HELP" is false.
   None of the four Group A runs reaches any combined frontier:
     0.6nt→1.7nt→4nt→8nt      93.3%,  9.9s mean
     0.6nt→1.7nt→4nt→14nt     94.5%, 10.9s mean
     0.6nt→1.7nt→4nt→4t       95.1%, 15.2s mean
     0.6nt→1.7nt→4nt→14t      98.2%, 30.9s mean
   The cheap prefix fires on 100% of tasks, so it raises P50 and mean
   immediately. It is not a free replacement for a single strong turn.

6. "DIMINISHING RETURNS" is metric-dependent.
   - Mean latency still gets large 4T gains because turn 4 only fires
     on a small hard remainder.
   - P50 barely moves because almost all frontier candidates already
     solve the median case before T4.
   - P90 sits in between: 4T helps, but only when the rescue path is
     both accurate and not too tail-heavy.

--- Why 4T sometimes helps and sometimes does not ---

1. 4T helps when T1-T3 already solve almost everything.
   The successful 4T configs all enter turn 4 rarely:
     - 4nt→8nt→14nt→4t   : T1+T2+T3 solve 95.3%; T4 only solves 2.9%
     - 4nt→8nt→14nt→14t  : T1+T2+T3 solve 95.7%; T4 only solves 3.1%
     - 1.7nt→4nt→14nt→14t: T1+T2+T3 solve 94.7%; T4 only solves 3.9%
   In these cases the expensive rescue turn is diluted over a tiny tail,
   so the frontier moves meaningfully.

2. 4T does not help when the extra structure is moved into the prefix.
   Group A adds 0.6nt and 1.7nt to every request, then still leaves
   14-18% of tasks for turns 3-4. Example:
     0.6nt→1.7nt→4nt→14t
       T1/T2/T3/T4/fail = 34.4% / 35.0% / 16.3% / 12.6% / 1.8%
       mean = 30.9s, p90 = 76.1s
   The early-turn overhead is paid by everyone, while the heavy 14t
   rescue still triggers often enough to destroy the tail.

3. 4T does not help when the rescue model is too expensive relative to
   the remaining error mass.
   4nt→8nt→1.7t→14t has excellent P50 (3.96s) because the median task
   exits before turn 3, but it is not a mean-frontier point:
     - turn 3 LLM latency = 50.1s
     - turn 4 LLM latency = 222.0s
     - turn 4 still handles 5.7% of tasks
   So it wins the typical case, but not the average case.

4. Stronger middle nothink beats weaker middle think.
   Compare the two Group D extensions:
     - 1.7nt→4nt→14nt→14t : 98.6%, 17.0s mean, 17.4s p90
     - 1.7nt→4nt→4t→14t   : 98.6%, 22.4s mean, 33.0s p90
   Same accuracy ceiling, much better latency with 14nt in turn 3.
   The stronger nothink turn removes enough tasks before the expensive
   final rescue to more than repay its own cost.

5. The best 4T recipe is: cheap nothink staircase first, sparse rescue
   last. The data now supports a simple rule:
     Use 4T only when the first 3 turns already reach ~95% accuracy.
     If the prefix is weaker than that, turn 4 fires too often and the
     extra turn becomes dead weight instead of a Pareto improvement.
